## 3. Work model and division-specific records

Keep four shared work types: Project, Program, Routine, Request. Tasks and Resources are reusable supporting capabilities; persistent Records describe members, partners, opportunities, content, assessments, agreements, transactions, knowledge, and award decisions.

- Project: bounded outcome, scope, milestones, deliverables, review, closure. Campaign and consulting engagement are configured project forms.
- Program: sustained initiative with cohorts/cycles, sessions, participants, assignments, and outcomes.
- Routine: recurring definition plus distinct scheduled occurrences. Weekly meeting attendance, fortnightly monitoring, and monthly close do not share one completion flag.
- Request: submitted need, handler, required information, review/state transitions, resolution. Legal, design, reimbursement, and IT have distinct forms and gates.
- Task: one canonical action with accountable owner, optional backup/contributors, date, status, and primary context. Personal, division, calendar, and portfolio views reuse its ID.
- Record: domain facts with explicit fields and permissions. A partner isn't a project; a grade isn't a task; a knowledge article isn't merely an external URL.

Prefer a few shared capability implementations with precise domain validation, rather than one excessively generic status workflow or unrelated task engines. Do not impose Project/Program/Routine/Request navigation steps on every user's daily work.

Project creation, including engagement/campaign project forms, requires an active CD/Co-Director-or-higher role and authorized target context. Ordinary members can propose tasks and accept cross-division work but cannot create a project merely through consultant participation or PL/PM assignment. Program/Routine/Request creation policies are separate; the new project rule must not silently restrict those types beyond their adopted permissions.

## 4. President and VP operating flows

### President

1. Open organizational attention view showing decisions awaiting authority, unresolved cross-VP handoffs, material deadlines, and current approved commitments. Counts link to permitted source records; no fabricated overall organization health score.
2. Open a decision packet with proposal, evidence, options, cost/resources, affected units, owner, constraints, and requested decision date.
3. Request clarification or record approve/hold/reject with rationale. Approval applies to a specific version; material changes require renewed decision.
4. Assign the responsible VP and next milestone. Receiving VP acknowledges or returns the handoff with missing information.
5. Review progress and exceptions through canonical division records rather than requiring repeated copy-paste status reports.
6. Record explicit resolution/escalation; term-end handover preserves unfinished commitments, decision history, and accountable successors.

### VPs

VP External coordinates Partner/Client acquisition and MarCom commitments; VP Internal coordinates people, financial/legal readiness, strategy, and internal capacity; VP Consulting coordinates delivery, knowledge, learning, and later expert support.

Each VP reviews reporting units, resolves dependencies within authority, forwards decisions requiring President/another VP, and acknowledges cross-VP commitments. An overdue task is not automatically an executive decision; escalation policy specifies owner, threshold, reason, and resolution. Regular VP reviews can be routines with agenda, minutes, and canonical follow-ups. Exact review cadence remains proposed.

## 5. External Engagement — Partner and Client

### Division-level responsibility

Shared external organization/contact identity, interaction history, owner/backup, opportunity pipeline, commitments, and handoffs. Partner and Client have distinct purposes and stage rules while reusing organization/contact records. One organization can be both without duplicate identities.

Primary view: editable operational table plus optional pipeline board. Useful columns: organization, opportunity kind, stage, PIC, backup, last interaction, next action/date, dependency, and agreement state. Last interaction is recorded evidence, not inferred from a status edit.

### Partner branch: relationship to active partnership

1. Identify a potential partner and search existing organizations/contacts before creating a record with source and responsible PIC.
2. Create partnership opportunity with objective, proposed mutual contribution, contact, suitability notes, expected milestones, and next action.
3. PIC prepares outreach and records actual attempt, channel, date, and outcome. Drafted and sent are different; the app does not pretend to send WhatsApp/email.
4. Record meeting, partner interest/constraints, proposed benefits, commitments, and next task. Backup handles a deliberately reassigned task; notifying a backup alone doesn't transfer accountability.
5. Prepare proposal with version, internal reviewer, contribution requests, and dependencies on MarCom, Consulting, FnL, or SnG.
6. Seek authorized commitment approval and Legal review where required. A pipeline stage cannot substitute for agreement/signature evidence.
7. Record negotiation and approved changes. Awaiting partner response, internally blocked, and declined have distinct explanations.
8. Record agreement outcome and actual obligations: owner, date, deliverable, counterpart commitment, review/renewal date.
9. Create/link delivery project/program when actual execution needs it. Receiving owner acknowledges the handoff; opportunity metadata and resources remain linked.
10. Maintain partner follow-ups, record fulfillment, review the relationship, and renew/close with reason. Closing an opportunity preserves the long-lived partner and prior interactions.

Recommended states: identified -> qualified -> contacted -> discussion -> proposal -> negotiation -> agreement pending -> active; lost/withdrawn/on hold as explicit branches. Exact names and required gates are proposed.

### Client branch: opportunity to Consulting handoff

1. Capture client need, source, contact, PIC, next action, confidentiality, and initial timing.
2. Qualify problem, expected output, available client information, budget indication if supplied, and decision maker. Unknown remains unknown.
3. Submit feasibility/scoping request to Consulting with named receiver and only permitted source material.
4. Project Associates/Consulting reviewer assesses fit, skill/capacity assumptions, rough scope, risks, and required clarification. This is not a unilateral Client promise of delivery.
5. Coordinate proposal using Consulting's approved scope version; FnL reviews legal/commercial readiness under the adopted process.
6. Record negotiation, scope changes, authorized commitment, signed evidence where applicable, and win/loss reason.
7. On accepted opportunity, link one Consulting engagement, canonical client identity, contacts, approved scope, communication responsibility, and open obligations. Receiver acknowledges handoff.
8. During delivery, Client tracks authorized relationship/account follow-ups; Project Associates tracks delivery. Decide who communicates which updates, preventing contradictory messages.
9. At completion, record relationship feedback and next opportunity while preserving the closed engagement's acceptance and financial states.

Screens: Relationships, Opportunities with Partner/Client filter, Follow-ups, Resources. Domain state is not automatically a top-level navigation tab. Branch-specific default filters are preserved on return.

## 7. HR

Confirmed scope: weekly meeting participant lists/absence notes; one monitoring-and-grading cycle every two weeks; Member of the Month. Recruitment, onboarding, member development, and offboarding remain recommended supporting scope, with SOP details to adopt.

### Weekly attendance and meeting records

1. Organizer creates actual meeting occurrence with unit, date/time, agenda, and expected participants. HR attendance role does not automatically make HR organizer of all meetings.
2. Capture expected roster from relevant current members/invitees; guests are separate, and unrelated members are not expected absentees.
3. Record absence notice against meeting ID with member, reason category, optional restricted explanation, and submission time. Notice and approved excuse are distinct.
4. Authorized officer records actual present/late/excused absence/unexcused absence/not required/not yet recorded. Status definitions, late threshold, and excuse approver require policy.
5. Reconcile roster, notices, and actual attendance; missing officer entry remains unknown rather than becoming unexcused absence.
6. Record minutes, decisions, and follow-up tasks. Shared minutes and attendance summary do not expose restricted explanations.
7. Finalize register with officer/date and exception notes. Corrections retain prior status and review lineage.
8. Link relevant finalized attendance revisions to fortnightly cycle. Attendance affects grades only through an adopted rubric.

### Fortnightly monitoring and grading

1. Configure consecutive 14-day windows anchored to an agreed date; submission/review/correction deadlines are independent policy fields. Every two weeks is not twice a month.
2. Capture eligible members, unit at assessment time, reviewers, leave/new-joiner/transfer exceptions, and rubric version. Reviewer coverage is checked before opening.
3. Member receives reporting item showing period, deadline, expected evidence, and criteria; submits achievements, work references, blockers/support needs, and next commitments.
4. Pull permitted actual task/deliverable/content/meeting/session references. Allow narrative evidence for work not recorded in app; no invented effort or quality from task counts.
5. Assigned unit/division reviewer verifies contributions, asks clarification, and drafts criterion scores/reasons against role-appropriate expectations.
6. HR checks completeness, consistent interpretation, exceptions, and unsupported scores. An alternate assesses reviewers and other conflicts.
7. Authorized reviewer finalizes reproducible scores and feedback against a specific rubric. Missing reports/criteria produce incomplete, not automatic zero.
8. Member sees own assessment, acknowledges or requests correction. Revision preserves original score/actor/time/evidence.
9. Create development action with owner/date: coaching, workload support, clearer expectations, resource help, or TnD referral. Referral is not automatic disciplinary action.
10. Close cycle with eligible/submitted/reviewed/incomplete/exempt/correction-pending counts. Open actions remain assigned after close.

Proposed rubric categories: quality, agreed delivery, collaboration/communication, development/contribution; scale/weights/anchors are open. For every score, record raw criterion values, maximums, weights, rubric version, sources, reviewer, and override reason. Different roles need equivalent expectations, not raw output-count ranking.

### Member of the Month

1. Open monthly recognition round with eligibility, included cycle IDs, nomination policy, panel, and decision/publication dates.
2. Gather finalized assessments and permitted contribution evidence. Recommended initial month attribution: cycles ending that month, with explicit coverage; final method remains open.
3. Resolve missing reviews/appeals or document authorized exceptions before treating shortlist as complete.
4. Panel reviews role-appropriate contribution evidence, nominations if adopted, conflicts, and tie rules. No arbitrary winner or automatic task-count maximum.
5. Record winner/decision/rationale/recusals; policy may support joint recognition or no award if adopted.
6. Prepare member-approved publishable name/photo/summary under adopted preferences, excluding private scores and support notes.
7. Create scoped MarCom publication request. Record actual announcement separately from decision.
8. Later score correction flags award for review rather than silently replacing published decision.

### Member lifecycle and development

Recruitment cycle -> application -> screening/interview -> decision -> invited account/onboarding -> active membership -> development -> transfer/leave/alumni. Each transition preserves identity, reviewer evidence, effective date, and unresolved duties. Offboarding previews tasks, approvals, resource/account custody, and required successor acknowledgment. HR onboarding and TnD training share participant references but have distinct completion requirements.

Screens: People; Monitoring & Development with weekly attendance/fortnightly cycles; Programs; Recognition. Candidate and private assessment details use narrower access. Members see own submissions/feedback/actions; reviewers see assigned cases; HR sees permitted organization-wide queues; leadership sees adopted aggregates and authorized detail.

## 8. Finance & Legal (FnL) — one division, two connected functions

FnL remains one director-led division under VP Internal. Legal and Finance are specialist queues/actions inside it, not separately invented divisions. Approval authority, numbering, signature, payment recording, and financial visibility remain separate permissions.

### Legal request and document lifecycle

1. Requester selects document type and submits purpose, parties, project/opportunity, required-by date, source materials, and requester/receiver.
2. Triage checks completeness and adopted SLA/calendar. Survey H-2 ordinary/H-3 agreement lead times are source proposals pending organizational policy; do not assume working/calendar-day basis or impose an automatic rejection without agreement.
3. Legal owner prepares draft version using permitted template and records review requirements. Urgency exception names approving authority and reason.
4. Review specific version, return revisions, and preserve each version/comment/decision.
5. Issue number through agreed unique register only at defined issuance stage; void/supersede retains old number and linked reason rather than silently reusing it.
6. Prepare signature-ready package; record each required signature's actual evidence/date separately. App approval is not a digital signature service.
7. Track applicable BAST/invoice-readiness prerequisites using actual evidence and independent review, not a project completion percentage.
8. Handoff permitted agreement/gate references to Finance with named receiver. Receiver accepts or returns missing information.
9. Archive signed/current/superseded states transparently; renewal/expiry follow-ups have owners and dates.

### Finance request, payment, and review

1. Set approved allocation by term/category/project with amount, basis, owner, and approving authority.
2. Requester submits funding/reimbursement with purpose, amount/currency, required date, category, project, and supporting evidence.
3. Finance verifies completeness, duplicates, policy, available allocation, and outstanding commitments. Return missing evidence without losing draft.
4. Authorized independent approver approves/rejects/revises requested amount with reason. Requester cannot approve their own claim.
5. Approval records commitment under adopted rules; it does not prove money moved.
6. Authorized recorder enters actual partial/full payment, date, amount, evidence, and remaining obligation. Paid totals derive from recorded transactions.
7. Refund/correction references original transaction and retains history; never delete payment evidence to make a chart fit.
8. Client receivable/term tracking, if adopted, uses a separate incoming-money basis linked to legal readiness; no automatic invoice generation or collection.
9. Monthly review reconciles allocation, approved commitments, payments, outstanding obligations, and evidence gaps. Exact bank/cash reconciliation process must be agreed; no full double-entry accounting claim.
10. Close review occurrence with snapshot/report basis and unresolved actions; export explains committed versus paid versus remaining and agrees with source transactions.

Screens: Requests with Legal/Finance views; Budget & Transactions; Documents & Register; Period Reviews. Budget formulas, approvals, and register are native authoritative records when adopted. Complex analysis can remain in Sheets; formulas/imports cannot become unreviewed payment/approval facts.

Screens: programs/cohorts; session schedule; participant/assignment table; review queue. HR governs organizational performance/recognition; TnD governs learning outcomes. Shared evidence does not imply duplicate grades or automatic awards.
